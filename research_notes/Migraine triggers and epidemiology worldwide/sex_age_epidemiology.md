# Migraine epidemiology by sex, age and hormonal life stage (worldwide, including GBD figures)

Method note: the web-fetch tool could not resolve PMC, Springer/BMC, Lancet or ncbi domains in this session, so the figures below come from search-result abstracts and snippets, not from full-text reading. Each figure is labeled with its GBD round (GBD 2019, GBD 2021 or GBD 2023) where relevant. Check exact numbers against the DOI or PubMed record before final publication.

## 1. Global prevalence and burden (GBD 2019, GBD 2021, GBD 2023, plus non-GBD pooled estimates)

### Takeaway
About 1.1–1.16 billion people have migraine, an age-standardized prevalence of about 14% that has stayed stable since 1990. Migraine is the second-leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) worldwide. Among young women it is the top cause of disability: first by DALYs in GBD 2019, with headache disorders second only to gynaecological diseases in females aged 15–49. The newest round, GBD 2023 (Lancet Neurology, Dec 2025), gives an age-standardized migraine prevalence of 14.1%: 17.6% in females and 10.5% in males.

### Cited Findings
**GBD 2019**
- Global age-standardized point prevalence of migraine in 2019 was 14,107.3 per 100,000 (95% UI 12,270.3–16,239), up 1.7% since 1990. Incidence was 1,142.5 per 100,000, up 2.1%. The YLD rate was about 525.5 per 100,000, up 1.5%. Prevalence was higher in females and rose with age up to 40–44, then fell (Safiri et al., *Pain* 2022;163(2):e293–e309) — [PubMed 34001771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34001771/)
- GBD 2019 countries with the highest age-standardized point prevalence: Belgium 22,400.6 per 100,000 (95% UI 19,305.2–26,215.8), Italy 20,337.7 and Germany 19,436.4 (Safiri et al. 2022) — [PubMed 34001771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34001771/); [UNIC record](https://pure.unic.ac.cy/en/publications/global-regional-and-national-burden-of-migraine-in-204-countries-/)
- GBD 2019 rank: migraine was the second-leading cause of disability (YLDs) worldwide for both sexes and all ages, and first among young women. Note that the "first in young women" ranking is by DALYs, not YLDs. In females aged 15–49, headache disorders had a DALY rate of 1,016.1 per 100,000, second only to gynaecological diseases (1,230.5) and ahead of depressive disorders (890.4). Among all young adults aged 15–49, headache disorders were the top cause of YLDs (813.4 per 100,000) (Steiner et al., *J Headache Pain* 2020;21:137, doi:10.1186/s10194-020-01208-0) — [PMC7708887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7708887/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10194-020-01208-0)
- GBD 2019 DALY rankings for headache disorders: 14th among all causes (both sexes, all ages), 10th in females, 3rd in young adults aged 15–49 (after low back pain and ischaemic heart disease) and 2nd in young adult females (after low back pain) (Steiner, Husøy, Stovner, *J Headache Pain* 2024;25:91, doi:10.1186/s10194-024-01795-2) — [PMC11145804](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11145804/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/443bbc70adce4ea2aa49878247d2d143)
- GBD 2019 regional YLD ranks for headache disorders: 3rd in East Asia & Pacific and in Middle East & North Africa, 2nd in Europe & Central Asia (Steiner 2020) — [PMC7708887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7708887/)

**GBD 2021**
- GBD 2021 Nervous System Disorders Collaborators (*Lancet Neurol* 2024, doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(24)00038-3): 3.40 billion people (43.1% of the world population) had a nervous-system condition. Migraine was among the top contributors to age-standardized DALYs, alongside stroke, neonatal encephalopathy and Alzheimer's disease. Secondary summaries report that about 1.16 billion people had migraine, and that migraine ranked first among nervous-system conditions in children and adolescents and second in adults under 60 — [summary](https://www.linksmedicus.com/?p=84906); [IHS call to action](https://ihs-headache.org/?p=11899)
- GBD 2021 global age-standardized migraine prevalence was 14,246.55 per 100,000 (95% UI 12,194.12–16,378.70). This comes from secondary analyses of GBD 2021 data; one gives 1.16 billion prevalent cases, or 14.7% of the population — [Pain Ther 2024, doi:10.1007/s40122-024-00690-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40122-024-00690-7); [PMC13529543](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13529543/)
- GBD 2021, by GBD region: age-standardized prevalence was highest in Latin America & Caribbean (18,595.34 per 100,000) and Western Europe (18,170.70). The age-standardized YLD rate was also highest there (679.39 and 676.64 per 100,000) — [Pain Ther 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40122-024-00690-7)
- GBD 2021, by SDI: middle-SDI regions had the most prevalent cases, incident cases and DALYs, while high-SDI regions had the highest rates — [J Headache Pain 2024, 15–39-year-olds, doi:10.1186/s10194-024-01832-0](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-024-01832-0)
- GBD 2021, women of childbearing age (15–49): an estimated 493.94 million prevalent cases in 2021, up 48% from 1990, with 33.33 million incident cases and 18.25 million DALYs. Middle-SDI regions held about one third of the global total — [J Headache Pain 2024, doi:10.1186/s10194-024-01798-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10194-024-01798-z)
- GBD 2021, China: the age-standardized prevalence in females (11,850 per 100,000) was 2.2 times that in males (5,390 per 100,000). China had 184.75 million prevalent cases in 2021, the most of any Asia-Pacific country compared (China, Australia, Japan, South Korea) — [PMC12683068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12683068/); [PMC12057080](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12057080/)
- GBD 2021, Brazil: one analysis reports Brazil had the highest country-level age-standardized prevalence (18,331 per 100,000) and incidence (1,489 per 100,000). This conflicts with GBD 2019, where Belgium was highest, and probably reflects a different comparison set or round — [Frontiers Neurol 2023, doi:10.3389/fneur.2023.1307413](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fneur.2023.1307413/full)

**GBD 2023 (newest round)**
- GBD 2023 Headache Collaborators (*Lancet Neurol* 2025;24(12):1005–1015, doi:10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00402-8): about 2.9 billion people had a headache disorder (95% UI 2.6–3.1), an age-standardized prevalence of 34.6%. The headache YLD rate was 541.9 per 100,000 (373.4–739.9), of which 487.5 (323.0–678.8) was attributed to migraine. Prevalence has been stable for three decades — [IHME](https://www.healthdata.org/research-analysis/library/global-regional-and-national-burden-headache-disorders-1990-2023); [Lancet Neurol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(25)00402-8/fulltext); [PubMed 41240916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41240916/)
- GBD 2023: global age-standardized migraine prevalence was 14.1% (12.1–15.9): 17.6% (15.2–19.9) in females and 10.5% (9.0–12.1) in males. Headache YLD rates were 739.9 per 100,000 in women and 346.1 in men. Medication-overuse headache made up 22.6% of migraine YLDs in males and 14.1% in females. GBD 2023 is the first round to use age- and sex-specific time-in-symptomatic-state estimates, from individual data on 41,653 people in 18 countries — [Lancet Neurol 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(25)00402-8/fulltext); [IHME](https://www.healthdata.org/research-analysis/library/global-regional-and-national-burden-headache-disorders-1990-2023)

**Non-GBD pooled estimate**
- Stovner, Hagen, Linde, Steiner, *J Headache Pain* 2022;23:34 (doi:10.1186/s10194-022-01402-2), drawing on 357 publications: global migraine prevalence 14.0% (95% CI 12.9–15.2), active headache disorder 52.0%, TTH 26.0%, headache on 15 or more days per month 4.6%. On any given day 15.8% of people have headache. Migraine was about twice as common in women; press coverage gives about 17% of women and about 8.6–9% of men (not verified in the abstract) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ffe6ba39997a4ba198e232d3ef714a87); [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/migraine/tension-headache/the-global-prevalence-of-headache-an-update-with-analysis-of-the/22641274); [HealthDay via MedicineNet](https://www.medicinenet.com/half_of_worlds_people_suffer_from_headaches/news.htm)

### Inferences
- GBD rounds and independent pooled estimates agree on about 14% age-standardized prevalence. The GBD 2023 female:male ratio of about 1.7 is smaller than the roughly 2–3:1 seen in population surveys. Age-standardizing across all ages, including children and older adults where the sex gap is small, compresses the ratio.
- Absolute case counts are rising (more than 48% growth since 1990 in women aged 15–49) while age-standardized rates are flat. The growth reflects population growth and ageing, not more migraine per person.
- Rates are highest in high-income Western Europe and Latin America. Whether East Asia is truly lower or is under-ascertained is unresolved.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the GBD 2021 YLD count for migraine or its exact rank by YLDs in women aged 15–49 from primary text. The GBD Results Tool (vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results) is needed for that.
- No GBD source confirmed which region has the lowest age-standardized migraine prevalence. East Asia is often described as lower, based on non-GBD surveys.
- The GBD 2019 global count (often quoted as about 1.1 billion) and its YLD count were not confirmed from primary text.

## 2. Female:male ratio by age, and when and why the sex difference appears

### Takeaway
Before puberty, prevalence is equal or slightly higher in boys. After menarche, girls overtake boys and the female:male ratio rises to about 2:1 by age 20 and about 3:1 or more around age 40. It narrows after menopause but stays near 2:1. Estrogen fluctuation, especially withdrawal, is the leading explanation.

### Cited Findings
- Victor, Hu, Campbell, Buse, Lipton, *Cephalalgia* 2010 (US NHIS 2003, n=40,892): migraine prevalence was 17.5% in females and 8.6% in males. Prevalence was bimodal, peaking in the late teens/20s and around age 50. Beyond age 10, females had higher prevalence. The ratio was highest in the reproductive years and about 2-fold after age 42 — [S4ME thread summarizing the paper](https://s4me.info/threads/migraine-prevalence-by-age-and-sex-in-the-united-states-a-life-span-study-2010-victor-et-al.49837/)
- Reviews report that before age 12 boys have equal or higher incidence and prevalence. After puberty migraine is 2–3 times more common in girls, prevalence peaks at ages 35–45, and the F:M ratio rises from 2:1 at age 20 to 3.3:1 at age 40 — [Frontiers Neurol 2024, doi:10.3389/fneur.2024.1441129](https://frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2024.1441129/full)
- AMPP and related work (Buse, Loder, Lipton et al.) summarize F:M ratios of 2:1 to 3:1, peaking in midlife — [MDedge review](https://mdedge.com/content/men-women-and-migraine-role-sex-hormones-obesity-and-ptsd); [Einstein record](https://einstein.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/sex-differences-in-the-prevalence-symptoms-and-associated-feature-2/)
- Children and adolescents: a systematic review of population studies found pooled migraine prevalence of 7.7% (95% CI 7.6–7.8), with girls more likely affected (OR 1.67, 1.60–1.75). This is Abu-Arafeh et al., *Dev Med Child Neurol* 2010 — [PubMed 20875042](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20875042/)
- Estrogen withdrawal hypothesis: first proposed by Somerville (1972) and the most accepted explanation for menstrual migraine, though a 2023 critical appraisal judged the supporting evidence "limited" (*J Headache Pain* 2023, doi:10.1186/s10194-023-01664-4) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s10194-023-01664-4)
- A 2024 cohort study (Mendelian-randomization style) found that later menarche lowered migraine risk, while oral contraceptive use and parity raised it ("cumulative exposure to estrogen may increase the risk of migraine") — [PubMed 38215242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38215242/)
- After puberty, migraine attacks in women are also more frequent, longer, more severe and more likely to relapse (MacGregor, *Continuum* 2015) — [MacGregor 2015 PDF](https://migrainecanada.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/PDFs_macgregor_menstrual_menopause_2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- The excess in women emerges at puberty and is greatest during reproductive years, then shrinks after menopause. This timing points strongly to cyclic ovarian hormones, estrogen withdrawal in particular, rather than to fixed genetic sex differences alone.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the AMPP 2007 primary sex-specific prevalence (often quoted as 17.1% F / 5.6% M). The closest verified AMPP figures are 17.3% F / 5.7% M, 11.8% overall (Buse et al.) — [Consultant360/AMPP summary](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/neurology/ahs/women-are-twice-likely-have-migraine). The American Migraine Study I (1989; 17.6% F / 5.7% M) and II (1999; 18.2% F / 6.5% M) figures are from memory and were not verified here.

## 3. Hormonal life stages

### Takeaway
Migraine onset clusters after menarche. Roughly 50–60% of women with migraine have menstrually associated attacks. Most women without aura improve markedly in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters of pregnancy, but migraine with aura often does not improve, and attacks commonly return in the first postpartum month. Perimenopause raises the risk of high-frequency headache. Natural menopause tends to bring improvement, while surgical menopause tends to bring worsening. Hormone therapy has mixed effects, depending on route and dose.

### Cited Findings
- Menarche: migraine onset is usually after menarche, in the teens and early 20s — [Springer chapter, Hormonal Influences on Headache](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-88-470-1700-9_9); later menarche lowers risk — [PubMed 38215242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38215242/)
- Menstrual migraine: more than 50% of women with migraine report a link with menstruation (MacGregor 2012 review) — [PMC3002599](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3002599); up to 60% (frovatriptan review) — [Dovepress](https://dovepress.com/article/download/16927)
- Pregnancy, from a prospective study (Sances et al., *Cephalalgia* 2003) of 47 women with migraine without aura: improvement in 46.8% in the 1st trimester, 83.0% in the 2nd and 87.2% in the 3rd. Complete remission occurred in 10.6%, 53.2% and 78.7%. Menstrually related migraine before pregnancy predicted less improvement — [PubMed 12662187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12662187)
- Pregnancy and aura: migraine with aura is less likely to improve or remit, and aura can appear for the first time in pregnancy — [Practical Neurology](https://practicalneurology.com/diseases-diagnoses/headache-pain/migraine-during-pregnancy/31515/); up to 9 in 10 improve by the end of pregnancy (Migraine Trust) — [Migraine Trust](https://migrainetrust.org/live-with-migraine/migraine-in-pregnancy/)
- Postpartum: migraine returned within the first week after delivery in 34.0% of women and within the first month in 55.3% (Sances 2003) — [PubMed 12662187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12662187)
- Perimenopause (Martin et al., *Headache* 2016, AMPP, n=3,664 women with migraine): the risk of high-frequency headache (10 or more days per month) was 60% higher in perimenopause and 76% higher in postmenopause than in premenopause. The design was cross-sectional, and medication overuse may contribute — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/01/160121130700.htm); [University of Cincinnati](https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/legacy/healthnews/2016/01/migraines-worsen-as-women-approach-menopause.html)
- Natural vs surgical menopause (Neri et al. 1993, summarized in Ripa et al., *Int J Womens Health* 2015 systematic review): migraine improved in 67% after spontaneous menopause versus 33% after surgical menopause, and worsened in 9% versus 67% — [PMC4548761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4548761/)
- Hormone therapy: a 2026 meta-analysis (2 studies, 22,614 postmenopausal women) found current HRT use was linked to higher migraine risk (RR 1.49; 95% CrI 1.12–1.99) — [Neurology Advisor](https://www.neurologyadvisor.com/news/estrogen-use-migraine-incidence-acog/). In a headache clinic sample, 64.1% reported improvement or remission on HT, 22.5% no change and 13.3% worsening. Transdermal estrogen is preferred because oral dosing causes fluctuations — [Expert Rev Neurother 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14737175.2023.2296610); [PMC4548761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4548761/)

### Inferences
- The pattern across life stages fits a "stable estrogen is protective, falling estrogen is a trigger" model. High, stable estrogen in late pregnancy brings improvement. Abrupt falls (menses, postpartum, oophorectomy) bring worsening. Erratic perimenopausal levels bring worsening. Low, stable levels after natural menopause bring improvement.

### Gaps
- The pregnancy trimester data come from one small Italian cohort (n=47). No large population-based estimate was found. Population-based prevalence of pure menstrual migraine (often quoted as about 7–14% of women with migraine for pure MM, about 20–22% of all female migraineurs for menstrually related MM) was not verified.

## 4. Age patterns: children, peak, decline, late onset

### Takeaway
Pooled child and adolescent prevalence is about 8–11%. Prevalence peaks around age 30–44: GBD 2021 puts the female peak at ages 40–44 (27.5%) and the male peak at ages 30–34. Prevalence declines after about age 50. New onset after 50 is uncommon and should prompt evaluation for secondary headache. In GBD 2021, migraine incidence peaks at ages 10–14.

### Cited Findings
- Onofri et al., *J Headache Pain* 2023 (meta-analysis, ages 8–18): migraine 11% (95% CI 9–14%), migraine without aura 8%, migraine with aura 3%, TTH 17% — [PubMed 36782182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36782182/)
- Abu-Arafeh et al. 2010: 7.7% pooled child and adolescent migraine prevalence, girls OR 1.67 — [PubMed 20875042](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20875042/)
- Austrian nationwide school study (ages 10–18): migraine 24.2%, much higher because of different case definitions. Headache 1-year prevalence was 82.1% in girls versus 67.7% in boys — [Onofri meta-analysis context / search summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36782182/) (primary Austrian paper not fetched)
- Finnish preschool study: migraine 4.3% in girls versus 3.6% in boys — [Frontiers Neurol 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2024.1441129/full)
- GBD 2021: incidence peaked at ages 10–14. Prevalence and DALYs peaked at ages 30–34 in both sexes in one analysis, while another GBD 2021 analysis reports the female peak at ages 40–44 (27.48%, 95% UI 22.28–34.32%) and the male peak at ages 30–34 — [PMC13529543](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13529543/); [Pain Ther 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40122-024-00690-7)
- GBD 2019: prevalence rose with age to 40–44, then declined (Safiri 2022) — [PubMed 34001771](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34001771/)
- East Asia: peak prevalence in women aged 30–49 was 11–20%, versus 3–8% in men (review of 41 studies from China, Japan and Korea) — [Takeshima et al., J Headache Pain 2019, doi:10.1186/s10194-019-1062-4](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-019-1062-4)
- GBD 2021 (children aged 5–14): a dedicated analysis with projections to 2050 exists — [PMC12446009](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12446009/) (figures not extracted). Migraine ranks first among nervous-system disorders in children and adolescents (GBD 2021) — [summary](https://www.linksmedicus.com/?p=84906)

### Inferences
- Sources disagree on the exact peak age (30–34 vs 40–44). The difference likely reflects sex-specific analysis: male prevalence peaks earlier, female prevalence later.

### Gaps
- I found no verified population figure for new-onset migraine after age 50 or 60. A prevalence in people over 65 (often quoted as about 3–7%) was not verified.

## 5. Large population studies (AMPP, AMS, Eurolight, Lifting The Burden, Asia)

### Takeaway
US surveys consistently show about 17–18% of women and 6–9% of men with migraine. Lifting The Burden studies show high prevalence in some low- and middle-income countries (India: 25.2%). Asian surveys report lower rates: Japan 8.4%, China 9.3%, Taiwan 9.1%.

### Cited Findings
- AMPP 2004 (US, about 120,000 households; Lipton et al., *Neurology* 2007;68:343–349): 11.8% met ICHD-2 migraine criteria, 17.3% of females and 5.7% of males (Buse et al. analysis). Probable migraine was 4.5% — [Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/neurology/ahs/women-are-twice-likely-have-migraine); [GHDx record](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/322198)
- US NHIS 2003: 17.5% of females and 8.6% of males (Victor 2010) — [S4ME](https://s4me.info/threads/migraine-prevalence-by-age-and-sex-in-the-united-states-a-life-span-study-2010-victor-et-al.49837/)
- Eurolight (Steiner et al., *J Headache Pain* 2014;15:31, doi:10.1186/1129-2377-15-31; n=8,271 across 10 EU countries): gender-adjusted 1-year migraine prevalence was 35.3%. All-migraine, including probable migraine, was 26.9% in males and 43.6% in females. These figures are inflated relative to other studies because of the sampling methods — [NTNU Open](https://ntnuopen.ntnu.no/ntnu-xmlui/handle/11250/2627055); [Table 3](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1129-2377-15-31/tables/3)
- Lifting The Burden, India (Karnataka; Kulkarni et al., *J Headache Pain* 2015;16:67): age-standardized 1-year migraine prevalence was 25.2%, higher in females — [PubMed 26197976](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26197976/). A newer nationwide Indian study (Delhi/NCR, 2024) also exists — [PubMed 38943062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38943062/)
- Japan (Sakai & Igarashi, *Cephalalgia* 1997;17:15–22; n=4,029): 1-year migraine prevalence was 8.4% — [doi:10.1046/j.1468-2982.1997.1701015.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1468-2982.1997.1701015.x). A claims-based study found 3.2%, using different methods — [J Headache Pain 2022, doi:10.1186/s10194-022-01439-3](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-022-01439-3)
- China (Yu et al., *Headache* 2012;52:582–591; n=5,041, door-to-door): 1-year migraine prevalence was 9.3% (95% CI 8.5–10.1) — [PubMed 22590713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22590713/)
- Taiwan (Wang et al., *Cephalalgia* 2000;20:566–572; Taipei): age- and sex-adjusted prevalence was 9.1%, with 14.4% in females and 4.5% in males — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1046/j.1468-2982.2000.00085.x)
- China, Japan and Korea review (41 studies): adult 1-year prevalence ranged from 6.0% to 14.3%, with low awareness and diagnosis — [Takeshima 2019](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-019-1062-4)

### Inferences
- In every region, women have about 2–3 times the migraine rate of men. Higher rates in some LMIC Lifting The Burden surveys suggest that East Asian figures may be genuinely lower, or may partly reflect methods (questionnaire vs interview, cultural reporting).

### Gaps
- No verified Korean population figure was found. The Korean Headache-Sleep Study (Kim et al. 2012, about 6.1%) is from memory and unverified. Other Lifting The Burden country results (Russia, Nepal, Pakistan, Ethiopia, Zambia) and Stovner's 2018 GBD 2016 *Lancet Neurology* figures (1.04 billion with migraine) were not retrieved in this session.
