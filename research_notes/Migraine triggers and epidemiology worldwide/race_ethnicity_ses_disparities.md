# Migraine Prevalence by Race, Ethnicity and Socioeconomic Status, and Disparities in Diagnosis and Treatment

Method note (Oct 2026): PubMed, PMC, Europe PMC and Wiley full texts could not be reached from this research environment (DNS/proxy blocks), so figures below come from web search results summarizing abstracts, journal landing pages and press materials. Items marked **[unverified in-session]** come from prior knowledge of the primary paper and should be checked against the cited PubMed/DOI link before publication.

## 1. US prevalence by race/ethnicity

### Takeaway
In US government surveys (NHIS, self-reported "severe headache or migraine" in past 3 months), American Indian/Alaska Native (AI/AN) adults have the highest prevalence (~18-19%). White, Black and Hispanic adults cluster around 14.5-16%, and Asian Americans are lowest (~9-11%). Older clinical-criteria studies (Stewart 1996, AMPP 2007) found *lower* prevalence in Black than in White Americans. The NHIS item does not use ICHD criteria, so the two kinds of study are not directly comparable.

### Cited Findings
- **NHIS 2015 (Burch, Rizzoli, Loder, Headache 2018;58:496-505, doi:10.1111/head.13281, PMID 29527677):** past-3-month prevalence of migraine or severe headache was highest in AI/AN adults (18.4%) and lowest in Asians (11.3%). Whites were 15.4% and Blacks 16.2% (as reported in secondary summaries). — [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/head.13281); [AJMC summary](https://www.ajmc.com/view/study-summary-a-targeted-systematic-review-to-determine-the-prevalence-of-migraine-and-severe-headache-in-the-united-states); [NHIS 2015 Table A-5](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/NHIS/SHS/2015_SHS_Table_A-5.pdf)
  - **Conflict:** an AHS "From the Journal" summary gives AI/AN at **19.2%** (Asians 11.3%), and one summary lists Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander at 20.3% as highest. 18.4% matches the NHIS 2015 table. The 19.2% figure probably comes from another year or table. — [AHS summary](https://americanheadachesociety.org/news/journal-prevalence-migraine-severe-headache); [MDLinx](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/june-is-migraine-and-headache-awareness-month-who-shoulders-migraine-burden/lfc-1732)
- **NHIS 2005-2012 average (Loder et al., Headache 2015;55:214-228, doi:10.1111/head.12506, PMID 25644596):** severe headache/migraine prevalence was Native American 17.7%, White 15.5%, Hispanic 14.5%, Black 14.45%, Asian 9.2%. The authors warn that broad categories such as "Asian" may hide meaningful subgroup differences. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25644596); [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/head.12506); figures via [eScholarship review](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1vm5c5c4/qt1vm5c5c4.pdf)
- **NHIS 2018 update (Burch, Rizzoli, Loder, Headache 2021;61:60-68, doi:10.1111/head.14024):** age-adjusted prevalence was 15.9% overall (21.0% of women, 10.7% of men) and has been stable for years. It gives estimates by age, sex and poverty status. The race-specific 2018 numbers were not retrievable. — [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/head.14024)
- **Kiarashi et al. 2021 summary:** migraine/severe headache affects White, Black and Latino people at roughly similar rates (~15%), with AI/AN people higher at ~19%. — [Neurology 2021;97:280-289, doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000012261](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/wnl.0000000000012261); [AAN press release](https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/4901)
- **AMPP (Lipton et al., Neurology 2007;68:343-349, doi:10.1212/01.wnl.0000252808.97649.21, PMID 17261680; 2004 mailed survey, n=162,576 aged ≥12, ICHD-2 criteria):** 1-year migraine prevalence was 11.7% (17.1% of women, 5.6% of men). Prevalence was significantly higher in Whites than Blacks: women 17.3% vs 13.7%, men 5.7% vs 4.1%. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17261680/)
- **Stewart, Lipton & Liberman, "Variation in migraine prevalence by race," Neurology 1996;47:52-59 (doi:10.1212/WNL.47.1.52, PMID 8710124):** a random-digit-dial telephone survey in Baltimore County, MD, using IHS criteria. **[unverified in-session]** Reported prevalence among women was White 20.4%, African American 16.2%, Asian American 9.2%. Among men it was White 8.6%, African American 7.2%, Asian American 4.2%. Racial differences persisted after adjustment for income and education. The authors suggested a genetic contribution because the pattern matched lower prevalence in Africa and Asia. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8710124/)
- Same Baltimore County group (Scher, Stewart, Liberman, Lipton): frequent headache was 33% more common in Caucasians (4.4%) than African Americans (3.3%). — [USUHS record](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/prevalence-of-frequent-headache-in-a-population-sample/)
- England (Steiner, Scher, Stewart et al., Cephalalgia 2003): migraine prevalence was higher in Caucasians than in other ethnic groups. — [Einstein record](https://einstein.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-prevalence-and-disability-burden-of-adult-migraine-in-england-2/)

### Inferences
- Two measurement traditions disagree on Black vs White prevalence. ICHD-criteria surveys (1996, 2004) find Black prevalence ~20-30% lower. The NHIS "severe headache or migraine" item finds Black ≈ White (~15-16%). The NHIS item captures non-migraine severe headache and depends on self-labeling, which in turn depends on prior diagnosis. Lower diagnosis rates in Black and Hispanic patients (Section 4) could also deflate criteria-based or "doctor-diagnosed" estimates. **Racial gaps in "diagnosed migraine" should not be read as true prevalence differences.**
- Asian Americans are consistently lowest across methods (9-11%), in line with lower prevalence in East Asia (Section 2).

### Gaps
- Exact 2018/2021 NHIS race-specific figures, and Black-White severity comparisons (attack frequency, disability) from Stewart 1996, could not be confirmed (full texts blocked).
- No US source was found that disaggregates Asian (e.g., South vs East Asian) or Hispanic subgroups for migraine specifically.

## 2. Global and ethnic/ancestry differences

### Takeaway
Modeled global estimates (GBD 2021) put migraine at ~1.2 billion people, with the highest age-standardized prevalence in Latin America/Caribbean and Western Europe. East and Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa are generally lower, but African data are sparse. Ancestry-diverse GWAS are still dominated by European samples, and they yield far fewer hits in African-ancestry groups, largely a power artifact.

### Cited Findings
- GBD 2021: 1.2 billion people with migraine and 2.0 billion with tension-type headache worldwide. — [GBD 2021 headache analysis, PMC12629824](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12629824/)
- GBD 2021: highest age-standardized migraine prevalence was in Latin America & Caribbean (~18,595/100,000) and Western Europe (~18,171/100,000). — [GBD 2021 global/regional migraine, PMC12620191](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12620191/)
- Ages 15-39: Tropical Latin America had the highest ASPR (27,542/100,000). South Asia had the largest absolute case count. East Asia showed the steepest rise in prevalence (AAPC 0.29) and Southeast Asia the steepest decline. — [J Headache Pain 2024, doi:10.1186/s10194-024-01832-0](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-024-01832-0)
- GBD 2023: North Africa & Middle East super-region had the highest age-standardized migraine YLD rate, and sub-Saharan Africa the lowest. — [Lancet Neurology 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/laneur/article/PIIS1474-4422(25)00402-8/fulltext)
- Sub-Saharan Africa "remains one of the large regions of the world with limited data directly derived from population-based studies" (Benin population study). — [PMC10996250](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10996250/)
- Pediatric meta-analysis: prevalence was higher in Europe (8.35%) and the Middle East (8.69%) than in Asia (6.70%) and the US (6.58%). — cited in [GBD youth analysis](https://thejournalofheadacheandpain.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s10194-024-01832-0) search summary (secondary)
- South Asia GBD 2021 trends paper. — [PMC11921782](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11921782/); Asia 1990-2021 — [PMC11892304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892304/)
- Stovner et al. 2022, "The global prevalence of headache: an update" (J Headache Pain), is the main pooled-survey alternative to GBD. Its regional tables were not retrieved. — referenced in search results; regional numbers not verified
- **Genetics:** Choquet et al. 2021 (Communications Biology) ran a multi-ancestry GWAS of >550,000 participants of European, East Asian, African American and Hispanic/Latino ancestry from GERA and UK Biobank, and found new loci. — [GenomeWeb](https://www.genomeweb.com/node/553701)
- Gasperi et al., Molecular Psychiatry Dec 2025 (Million Veteran Program): multi-ancestry meta-GWAS of >433,000 veterans (87,859 cases), with 49 genome-wide significant loci (36 novel). SNP heritability was 10% in men and 16% in women. — [PMC12863721](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12863721/)
- An AAN 2025 conference report on an MVP analysis (90,600 cases) found 624 significant SNPs in the European group vs 3 in African Americans, 8 in Hispanics and 59 in Asians, and 789 SNPs pan-ancestry. These are conference SNP counts that do not match the published locus counts. — [EMJ](https://www.emjreviews.com/neurology/news/landmark-migraine-study-identifies-778-genetic-risk-variants-in-veterans-aan-2025/)

### Inferences
- The fewer GWAS hits in African-ancestry samples mainly reflect smaller sample sizes. They are not evidence of lower genetic susceptibility.
- GBD figures are modeled. Regions with sparse data (Africa, parts of Asia) carry high uncertainty, and low estimates may partly reflect under-ascertainment or survey method rather than true biology.

### Gaps
- No verified country-level survey figures were retrieved for specific East Asian (e.g., Japan, Korea, China), South Asian (India), Middle Eastern or Latin American national studies. The researcher on global epidemiology should cover these.
- No robust evidence was found that separates ancestry from environment and SES for ethnic differences in prevalence.

## 3. Socioeconomic status (income, education, employment)

### Takeaway
In the US, migraine prevalence rises as household income falls. AMPP incidence/remission modeling supports **social causation**: higher incidence in low-income groups, with equal remission across groups. NHIS shows high prevalence among unemployed adults and those with family income below $35,000.

### Cited Findings
- **Stewart, Roy, Lipton, Neurology 2013;81:948-955 (doi:10.1212/WNL.0b013e3182a43b32; AMPP data 2004-):** prevalence increased as household income decreased in both sexes, across income groups <$22,500, $22,500-59,999 and ≥$60,000. The gradient was not explained by race or other confounders. Higher **incidence** in low-income groups explained most of the variation, while **remission did not differ by income**. This is consistent with social causation, not social selection (downward drift). — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3182a43b32); [accompanying editorial](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3182a43ea7)
- AMPP (Buse et al., Headache 2013, doi:10.1111/head.12150): prevalence fell as income rose. In households with income ≥$90,000, 13.6% of females and 4.2% of males had migraine. The comparable low-income figures were not reliably extractable. — [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/head.12150)
- Stewart et al., JAMA 1992 (American Migraine Study I, 1989 data): an early report that migraine prevalence was inversely related to household income. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/21428983_Prevalence_of_Migraine_Headache_in_the_United_States_Relation_to_Age_Income_Race_and_Other_Sociodemographic_Factors)
- **NHIS 2015 (Burch 2018):** prevalence was 21.4% among unemployed adults who had previously worked, 16.6% among those who never worked, and 19.9% among those with family income below $35,000 (vs 15.3% overall). — [AJMC summary](https://www.ajmc.com/view/study-summary-a-targeted-systematic-review-to-determine-the-prevalence-of-migraine-and-severe-headache-in-the-united-states); [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/head.13281)
- NHIS-based reporting: women and uninsured people are more likely to report migraine. — [MedicalResearch.com](https://medicalresearch.com/women-and-uninsured-more-likely-to-suffer-from-migraines/)
- Korea: SES has a sex-specific influence on migraine and TTH prevalence (Korean Headache Survey). — [PMC3850998](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3850998/)
- Equity framing: Befus et al., "A Critical Exploration of Migraine as a Health Disparity," Curr Pain Headache Rep 2018. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11916-018-0731-3)
- Tertiary center (Stanford): during the CGRP-drug rollout, the 2nd and 3rd zip-code income quartiles had the lowest prescription rates, and race/ethnicity was a significant factor in CGRP mAb/gepant prescription. — [Neurology Clinical Practice, doi:10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200517](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/CPJ.0000000000200517)

### Inferences
- The SES gradient also confounds racial comparisons. NHIS similarity between Black and White prevalence despite lower Black income could mean that a lower criteria-defined prevalence is offset by SES, or it could reflect measurement differences.
- Reverse causation (disability reducing income) is plausible for prevalent cases. The AMPP finding of equal remission argues against selection as the main driver.

### Gaps
- No verified figures by education level (US) were retrieved.
- Low-/middle-income countries have little SES-gradient data.

## 4. Disparities in diagnosis, treatment, specialist access and trial representation

### Takeaway
Black and Hispanic Americans with migraine are consistently less likely to be diagnosed and to use migraine care. Evidence on triptan prescribing *once seen* is mixed: national NAMCS 2006-2013 found no significant racial difference, while 2025 survey data and pediatric ED data show gaps. Newer CGRP-targeted drugs show prescribing gaps by race. Black and Hispanic participants are under-represented in trials.

### Cited Findings
**Diagnosis and care use**
- **Nicholson et al., Headache 2006;46:754-765 (doi:10.1111/j.1526-4610.2006.00453.x):** primary care waiting-room sample, n=131 with migraine and moderate-severe disability (77 African American, 54 Caucasian). African Americans were less likely to use health care for migraine (46% vs 72%, P<.001) and less likely to have received a headache diagnosis (47% vs 70%, P<.001). — [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1526-4610.2006.00453.x); [PMC2443411](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2443411/)
- **Acute medication, 14% vs 37%:** "only 14% of Black headache patients receive prescriptions for acute migraine medications, compared to 37% of white headache patients" (American Migraine Foundation; also cited in Kiarashi 2021 press coverage). This most likely comes from the Nicholson 2006 cohort, which is small and single-site. — [AMF](https://americanmigrainefoundation.org/resource-library/racial-disparities-in-migraine-care/); [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/headaches-are-vastly-undertreated-among-racial-and-socioeconomic-groups)
- **Loder et al., Headache 2015 (NAMCS/NHAMCS):** migraine-diagnosis ambulatory visits per 10,000 population were Hispanics 89.5, Whites 176.3, Blacks 133.2, whereas nonspecific headache visit rates were similar across groups. The authors suggest Hispanic underdiagnosis and lower care-seeking by Black patients (hypotheses). — [PubMed 25644596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25644596)
- **Kiarashi, VanderPluym, Szperka et al., Neurology 2021;97:280-289 (doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000012261):** review covering race/ethnicity, SES and geography. As reported in press coverage: Latino patients were ~50% less likely to receive a migraine diagnosis than White patients; Black patients presenting to EDs with headache were 4.8 times less likely to be imaged; nonwhite children were less likely to receive headache medication and 3 times less likely to receive imaging; Black men received the least headache care of any group. Drivers named include discrimination, insurance, specialist shortages, under-representation in research, and the false belief that Black people tolerate pain better. Proposed fixes include telemedicine, social-needs screening, education, and recruiting under-represented clinicians. (Press figures; one HealthDay account instead says Black patients are 25% less likely to be diagnosed, so sources differ.) — [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/wnl.0000000000012261); [AAN press](https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/4901); [UT Southwestern](https://www.utsouthwestern.edu/newsroom/articles/year-2021/headaches.html)
- **Pediatric ED (Kellier et al., Neurology 2025;104(5), doi:10.1212/WNL.0000000000213351; PHIS, 49 children's hospitals, 2016-2022, ages 5-21):**
  - Migraine diagnosis was given to 45.5% of non-Hispanic White, 28.2% of non-Hispanic Black and 28.3% of Hispanic children (p<0.001).
  - Black children had lower adjusted odds of MRI (aOR 0.56, 95% CI 0.46-0.69).
  - Black children were 37% more likely, and Hispanic children 54% more likely, to receive only oral (no IV) medication.
  - [Neurology](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000213351); [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/study-reveals-gaps-in-migraine-diagnosis-treatment-for-black-and-hispanic-children)

**Acute and preventive prescribing**
- **Charleston & Burke, Cephalalgia 2018 (doi:10.1177/0333102417716933; NAMCS 2006-2013, ~50 million weighted migraine visits):** any Level A abortive (triptan or DHE) was given to 15.3% of African American, 19.4% of non-Hispanic White and 17.7% of Hispanic patients (p=0.64). The authors found no major racial/ethnic differences in abortive or preventive treatment. Level A acute medications were underused and opioids overused across all groups. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0333102417716933)
- **Migraine Report Card survey (J Racial Ethnic Health Disparities 2025, PMID 40767929):** Black and Hispanic respondents reported less use of prescription acute and preventive migraine medication than White respondents despite similar insurance status. Preventive prescription use was 20% in White, 7% in Black and 10% in Hispanic respondents. Subgroups were small (46 Black, 75 Hispanic), and the gaps were most marked in high-frequency migraine with medication overuse. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40615-025-02577-5); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40767929/)
- **CGRP mAbs and gepants (Stanford Headache Clinic, AHS 2024 abstract; 1,434 adults with chronic migraine, mAbs 2018-2022, gepants 2020-2022):** White patients received significantly more prescriptions than other racial groups, and men more than women. The study was not designed to determine causes. — [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/racial-gender-inequities-cgrp-gepant-prescriptions-2024a1000blc)
- Lasmiditan trials: post hoc analysis found migraine history and response were comparable across racial/ethnic groups. — [Curr Med Res Opin 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03007995.2022.2057152)

**Trial representation**
- **Robbins et al., Headache 2017 (doi:10.1111/head.13018, PMID 28127754):** 36 migraine RCTs (from 2011, n≥50). Only ~69% reported race/ethnicity. White participants averaged ~83% of enrollees and were over-represented relative to the general and migraine populations. Non-Whites (and men) in US trials were statistically under-represented. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28127754/); [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/head.13018)
- Pooled rimegepant US trials: 696/3,551 (19.6%) participants were Black and 76.0% were White. — [PMC13044557](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13044557/)
- US behavioral migraine-prevention trials (AHRQ review): Black participants were ~13.6% of adult trial participants (race reported in only 14 of 25 trials). — [NCBI Bookshelf NBK608525](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK608525/)
- "Eleven things to facilitate participation of underrepresented groups in headache medicine research" (Headache 2023). — [PMC10091268](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10091268/)

**Professional society statements**
- An AHS/AMF position statement (~2020) recognized systemic racism as a cause of racial health disparities, and AHS created a Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Task Force. — [AMF: Racial Disparities in Migraine Care](https://americanmigrainefoundation.org/resource-library/racial-disparities-in-migraine-care/)
- Charleston et al., "Equity of African American Men in Headache in the United States: A Perspective From African American Headache Medicine Specialists (Part 1)," Headache 2020. — [PubMed 33140448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33140448)
- Discrimination and quality of life: perceived discrimination was associated with worse migraine-specific quality of life in a Black/African American sample. — [PubMed 40454748](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40454748/)
- Indian Health Service headache-care perspective (AI/AN access). — [PubMed 37171379](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37171379/)

### Inferences
- The most consistent disparity sits upstream: care-seeking, diagnosis and ED diagnostic labeling. Once a migraine visit is coded, national prescribing differences for triptans were not significant (2006-2013). Disparities in recent, costly therapies (CGRP drugs) and in patient-reported preventive use point to access, cost and prescriber factors.
- The pediatric ED data, where White children were labeled "migraine" 45.5% vs ~28% for Black and Hispanic children, are a direct example of **diagnostic labeling bias**. Diagnosis-based prevalence data (claims, EHR, NAMCS) will understate migraine in minority groups.
- Several widely repeated figures (14% vs 37%; 46% vs 72%) trace back to one small 2006 single-site study and should be cited with that caveat.

### Gaps
- No triptan-prescribing-by-race study was found from Loder 2012 specifically. The request may refer to Loder et al. 2015 (Headache), which is cited above.
- Race-specific odds ratios for the Stanford CGRP/gepant analysis were not retrievable.
- No verified national data on neurologist or headache-specialist visit rates by race.
- No recent formal AHS health-equity consensus statement (2023-2026) was found beyond the ~2020 AHS/AMF statement.

## 5. Sexual and gender minorities (transgender people on hormone therapy)

### Takeaway
Evidence is sparse and mostly small cross-sectional studies. Estrogen-based feminizing therapy is associated with migraine prevalence similar to cisgender women's in the small Dutch study. Newer studies find no significant differences between estrogen and testosterone users, or between trans men and cis women.

### Cited Findings
- **Pringsheim & Gooren, "Migraine prevalence in male to female transsexuals on hormone therapy" (Neurology 2004;63:593-594) [unverified in-session citation details]:** among trans women on anti-androgen plus estrogen, migraine prevalence was 26%, vs 25% in cisgender women and 7.5% in cisgender men, with visual aura in 54% of those with migraine. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8405888_Migraine_prevalence_in_male_to_female_transsexuals_on_hormone_therapy)
- AAN abstract (P14-12.004): 135 trans women (85% on estrogen) had 19% migraine prevalence, and 135 trans men (84% on androgens) had 20%. There was no cisgender comparator. — [Neurology abstract](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000203915)
- Brazil case-control: trans men on testosterone vs cisgender women showed no differences in migraine frequency, headache type or severity. — [PubMed 40251852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40251852/)
- **Zavala et al., Headache 2026 (doi:10.1111/head.15076), Argentina cross-sectional:** migraine/probable migraine was found in 50.0% of estrogen users vs 44.3% of testosterone users (p=0.767, not significant). — [Wiley](https://headachejournal.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/head.15076); [PMC12951696](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12951696/)

### Inferences
- Data are consistent with estrogen exposure shifting trans women toward female-typical migraine prevalence, but samples are small and selection-prone.

### Gaps
- No population-based prevalence data exist for sexual-minority (LGB) adults in this search, and none on access or treatment disparities for gender-minority people with migraine.
